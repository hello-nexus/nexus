## Highlights

- **Sensor reactive lighting.** A new Sensor reactive category on the Lighting page's Animation tab has effects driven by your PC's own readings: Load bar follows CPU load, Liquid fill and Segment meter follow memory use, and Color shift and Rising heat follow CPU temperature. Pick a different reading for any of them under Sensor in the effect's settings, and their colors come from the color wheel, with a Reverse colors switch.
- **Lighting uses less CPU on Windows.** Lighting effects now render through Direct3D 11, so on a PC with Intel integrated graphics they no longer keep a CPU core busy in the background. Switching effects is smoother too: the current effect keeps playing while the next one loads.
- **Music Reactive can listen to a microphone.** On Windows, Music Reactive lighting can follow a microphone or any other input instead of what your PC plays. Pick it under Music Reactive source in Settings > Lighting & Cooling, or from the picker beside the music button on the Lighting page.
- **You choose which competing apps Nexus terminates.** When apps that drive the same lighting, fans or peripherals are running, during setup or from the Conflicting apps detected alert, each app is a card you click to choose whether Nexus terminates it or it keeps running. Each card shows the devices it competes for and what will happen to it, an app that starts with Windows can be removed from startup, and nothing changes until you press Terminate or Keep all running.

## Lighting

- Game Sync has been removed due to compatibility issues, and we may bring it back in an improved form in the future. If your lighting was on Game Sync, it switches to Static. On Windows, the Game Sync tab stays visible but disabled with this note, and Remove tab takes it away.
- Picking an effect, a mode tab or a color in Advanced mode while lighting is Off turns it back on for all your devices.
- Simple mode no longer takes back devices you turned Nexus Control off for, and neither does choosing Off or finishing lighting setup. A Control all devices button at the top right of the Lighting page brings every device back when you want it.
- Customize device has a new Frame cap that limits how often that device's lighting updates (Off, or 30, 20, 15, 10, 5 or 1 Hz), for a device or PC that stutters during animated effects. It is off by default, and your choice stays with the device when you switch presets.
- Render GPU in Settings > Lighting & Cooling is now called Rendering device. On Windows it can also pick your CPU ("software rendering"), which frees up the GPU in exchange for higher CPU load.
- On a PC with Intel integrated graphics and a dedicated graphics card, Auto no longer moves lighting to the dedicated card, since rendering on the integrated GPU no longer costs a CPU core.
- Screen Mirror lighting updates at 30 fps, the same rate as every other effect.
- On Windows, opening the Mirror tab while another mode is running shows a live preview of the selected monitor, and effect previews on the Lighting page load faster after your first visit.
- In Effect Studio, a knob can now move with a sensor as well as with music or a wave. Starters opens a picker with thumbnails grouped by category, and settings that are either on or off, such as Flip, are switches.
- On a new install, every device starts out covering the whole lighting canvas, so each one shows the full effect. Existing layouts are not changed.
- Hovering a mode tab on the Lighting page says what it does, such as "Animated effects, or build your own in Effect Studio" for Animation.
- Pins explain themselves. The first time you pin a device on the Lighting page, a hint at its pin says to keep it selected to change its lighting, and to unpin it to go back to the lighting for all devices. Hovering a pin says that modes only change that device while it is selected, and Pin and Unpin in a card's right-click menu describe what each does.
- On a new install, these mice start with Nexus Control off, because lighting control stops their buttons or DPI from working: Corsair Katar Pro, Katar Pro V2 and Katar Pro XT, Corsair M55 RGB PRO, Corsair M65 RGB Ultra Wired, Corsair Scimitar PRO RGB and HyperX Pulsefire Haste. Turn a mouse's card on to light it anyway. Existing installs keep them as they were.
- Newly supported: MSI MEG X870E GODLIKE MAX and MSI MEG Z790 GODLIKE, including the Z790's MEG logo and the lights below the riser card and along the bottom edge. Thanks @GrPK!

## Cooling

- Hovering a mode tab on the Cooling page says what it does, from Silent ("Slow, quiet fans") to Max ("Every fan at 100%, always").
- A pinned fan shows a pin you can click to unpin it, and hovering it says why it is pinned: a fan you pinned keeps its own speed when you switch modes, while GPU fans, pumps and AIO coolers start pinned because modes follow CPU temperature, pump speed stays as set, and the cooler runs its own curve. Pin and Unpin in a fan's menu describe what each does.

## Monitoring

- A new Read RAM temperatures switch in Settings > Monitoring & Diagnostics turns off reading the temperature sensor on each memory stick. It is on by default; turn it off if your PC fails to restart after installing Nexus.

## Devices

- A device with Nexus Control off can be hidden from the sidebar: right-click its row and choose Hide from sidebar. Its device page has a Show in sidebar switch to bring it back.

## Windows

- Settings > General has a Fast Startup switch. Fast Startup starts Windows faster after Shut down, but USB devices are not fully reset, so turn it off if a device stops responding or is not detected after startup.

## Accounts and privacy

- Benchmark results are no longer uploaded to the public leaderboard on their own. After a run, the results ask "Share this result on the public leaderboard?", and nothing is uploaded unless you choose Publish.
- Signing in to an account whose email you have not verified offers Resend email, with a short wait between sends.

## Fixes

- Some PCs could fail to restart after installing Nexus, because the memory's temperature sensor was left in a state the PC did not expect. Nexus now puts it back when it shuts down.
- On Windows, the window shown while an update installs could stay on screen after the update had finished.
- Windows Defender could flag the window Nexus shows while it installs an update.
- A monitor turned into a panel could lose its layout when Windows gave it a new display id, for example after moving it to another port or reinstalling the graphics driver.
- Lighting and Cooling on a touch panel could drop back to a single selected device after a restart or a device rescan.
- On a touch panel, a palm resting on the static color grid could take the pick from your fingertip.
- On a zoomed touch panel, picking a static color could select a different color from the one under your finger.
- When editing a widget on a panel, its settings sheet could cover the widget or sit stranded at the bottom of the screen.
