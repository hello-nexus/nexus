## Highlights

- **Third-party devices wait for their own app.** A Corsair, Lian Li or NZXT device you turn on in Nexus now waits while iCUE, L-Connect or NZXT CAM is running, and Nexus takes it over as soon as that app closes. Stream Deck and Tryx devices wait for the Elgato app and Kanali the same way. Corsair and Lian Li hubs are never turned on for you, and on a fresh install these devices start with Nexus Control off and their apps left running.
- **Recover a HYTE device stuck in update mode.** If a firmware update is cut short, a cooler such as the HYTE Q60 can stay in update mode with its fans at full speed. Nexus now spots this, shows a banner on the Cooling page and in Firmware Updates, and Recover reinstalls the firmware in about a minute.
- **Panel presets that follow your apps.** Every panel's page has a presets menu: save the layout, theme, background and widget styling as up to 10 named presets, and use Trigger with apps to load one while a game or app is in focus, like lighting presets.
- **Widget text that reads on any background.** Panels have a new Text color setting. Adaptive, the new default, gives each widget white or black text depending on what is behind it, so text stays readable over bright photos, videos and effects. Theme keeps the previous look, and Custom paints all widget text one colour.

## Devices

- A device card on the Devices page shows when the app that competes for it is running. Turning Nexus Control on while that app runs asks to end it first, instead of letting both drive the device.
- Corsair iCUE LINK hubs: Nexus keeps its connection to the hub open between lighting frames and backs off when the hub is busy, instead of reconnecting over and over.
- A monitor used as a panel and then turned off leaves the Devices list; add it again from Devices > Displays. The Corsair Xeneon Edge keeps its row.

## Panels

- Wallpaper Engine users (Windows): once Wallpaper Engine is running, a HYTE Y70 or a monitor used as a panel switches its Backdrop to Desktop, so the animated wallpaper shows behind the widgets. A Backdrop you already picked is kept, and a Y70 in Compatibility mode keeps the wallpaper. Not yet verified on hardware.
- Marketplace apps such as Aquarium fill the whole round screen of coolers like the NZXT Kraken instead of a smaller square inside it.
- Marketplace apps such as Aquarium take about half the processing time to animate.
- When a gallery photo covers the whole screen, the background effect or video behind it pauses on single-widget screens like the Q60 and cooler LCDs.
- Calculator keys are borderless with larger labels and proper math symbols.
- The swipe-up hint on touch panels has a longer arrow and a slower motion.

## Profiles and account

- Marketplace app data, such as your Aquarium, now belongs to each profile. A new profile starts fresh, and switching profiles switches the app's data too.
- Export saves a readable .nexusprofile file that includes app data. Import and restoring from the cloud show which apps' data come along and let you leave it out.
- Profiles go to your account only when you press Back up now. The button turns into a Backed up check when it lands, every profile shows the new backup time even if nothing in it changed, and a failed backup shows why.
- Cloud profiles show when each was backed up and which system it came from, and the active profile is highlighted.
- Your username can be changed once every 30 days, and the message says when you can change it next.
- Computers are called systems throughout the app; your account lists them under My Systems.

## Benchmark

- A new run screen: one column per part rises to its score as each test finishes, then your Nexus Score counts up.
- Results show your score on a banner with a Latest/Best picker, and a scrollable history of every run with its date and time.
- Hardware lists wrap long part names instead of cutting them off.
- Benchmark tools close with Nexus instead of running on in the background after a crash (Windows).

## Updates

- macOS and Linux install Nexus updates from inside the app, like Windows. This starts with the next update after this one, so this version is still a manual download on those systems.
- The update badge appears even when an update cannot download in the background, and opens the update window.
- After Download & install, the update window moves to the new version as soon as Nexus restarts instead of sitting on "Starting...".

## Coming from Nexus 2

- Nexus closes HYTE Nexus 2 every time it starts, since both drive the same devices. Onboarding has an Uninstall HYTE Nexus 2 switch, on by default.
- Moving from Nexus 2 brings over the HYTE Y70's background (your upload, a downloaded preset or the default particles video), your primary colour as the accent, and widget opacity. Video backgrounds have not yet been verified on hardware.

## Also in this release

- An app's Marketplace page shows what's new in the version on offer.
- Installed Battle.net games are recognised for FPS history (Windows).

## Fixes

- An update that could not be verified left the update window on a progress bar that never moved and could not be closed.
- Quitting Nexus on macOS with a dashboard open could take 30 seconds.
- A HYTE device stuck in update mode showed up as an NP50 and a MiniHub.
- The Diagnostics widget preview in the widget picker showed a GPU throttling warning.
