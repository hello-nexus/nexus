## Highlights

- **Brightness that follows the sun.** A new Daylight sync switch in the brightness schedule dims your lighting around sunset and brings it back up around sunrise, using today's sun times where you are. Open it from Settings > Lighting & Cooling > Brightness schedule, or from the clock beside the brightness slider on the Lighting page.
- **Lian Li wireless fans stay bound.** Fans and Strimers that dropped off a Lian Li L-Wireless Controller could stay unbound for hours. A new Lock bound devices switch on the controller's Devices tab, on by default, binds them back to the controller by itself whenever they drop.
- **Lian Li LCD Flex fans over USB.** On Windows, Lian Li TL LCD Flex and SL-INF LCD Flex fans plugged in by USB get their own device page, with their built-in effects, per-zone lighting and sync across fans. Their speeds join the Cooling page, and each fan's screen can show a Nexus panel or your own pictures and videos.
- **Lighting that reaches sparse LEDs.** Customize device has a new Sampling setting. Each LED can read the area of the effect nearest to it instead of a single point, so effects that pass between sparse LEDs, such as on a keyboard lit only around its edge, are no longer missed.

## Lighting

- With Daylight sync on, the schedule runs from a night level to a day level, with sunrise and sunset marked on the graph. Drag the night or day line to set each one (10% and 100% to start). Location can be Auto, one of your saved weather locations, or any place you search for. On a day the sun does not rise or set there, your own curve applies.
- Daylight sync starts on for a schedule still on its default curve. A curve you drew yourself keeps running as drawn until you turn Daylight sync on.
- Follow the schedule is now the first switch in the Brightness schedule window, and turning it off dims the settings below it instead of hiding them.
- Sampling has four choices. LED reads a single point, as before. Nearest gives each LED the part of the effect closest to it, and Rows and Columns split the effect into horizontal bands or vertical columns, one area per LED. With any of the last three, each LED's area shows as an outline on the Lighting page and in Customize device. Each device keeps its own choice.
- When another app that drives the same hardware is running, a strip under the device's card on the Lighting page names it. It reads "Controlled by" the app when Nexus is not driving that device, and turns amber when both are. Click the strip to open Conflicting apps detected for just those apps.
- Fan and strip headers on OpenRGB controllers such as the Corsair Commander Core get an LED count, so colors land on the LEDs you actually have plugged in. On the Devices page's Available tab, each OpenRGB device with a resizable header has an LED counts section with one slider per header, applied when you let go, and a note asks you to set the headers that have no count yet. Devices still waiting for a count now show up instead of staying hidden: on the Lighting page they link there with Set LED counts, and the setup screen reads Set LED counts after setup.
- Lian Li TL Flex and SL-INF Flex fans on an L-Wireless Controller now place their LEDs on the Lighting page where they really sit on each fan, so effects move across them the way they look.

## Panels

- Any panel widget can be resized from its corners. Right-click a widget, or press and hold it on a touch screen, and choose Resize, then drag a corner. The widget snaps to the sizes it supports, and the widgets next to it move aside on the same page. On a panel's device page, the corners also show when you hover a widget.
- Web View and Whiteboard can grow past 4x4 to larger even sizes, up to what the panel holds, and Web View has a new 4x2 size.
- Settings > Profiles has a new Panels category for the layouts, looks and presets of your panels and cooler displays. It starts out Shared, so every profile shows the same panels. Set it to Per-profile and each profile keeps its own panel looks and switches them with the profile. Exported profiles carry them, and importing a profile while Panels is Shared adds its panel looks as presets on matching panels, leaving the look you have in place.
- Panel layouts and cooler display content are saved with your profile, so they come back if Nexus starts with its settings reset, for example after a crash.
- The Sensor reactive lighting effects, such as Load bar and Rising heat, can now be a panel's animated background, following your PC's readings live. On a panel's device page, Background mode > Animations has a search box.
- Monitoring widgets have a new Square Ring design, which runs the ring around the tile's rounded corners and also fills wide or tall tiles.
- A Deck key's custom icon can now be an animated GIF or an MP4 or WebM video, up to 8 MB. On a key's Icon, the Custom tab now has Upload Image or Video next to GIFs, and a cropper frames the picture or clip first. The icon plays on panel Deck widgets, and a Stream Deck key shows a still frame of it.
- When you import from Nexus 2, a HYTE Y70 dock tile's custom image or video now comes along as its Deck key icon.
- The OBS widget's buttons now use the same key style as the Calculator widget, and its stream and recording timers count up every second.

## Devices

- While Lock bound devices is on, Bind and Unbind on the L-Wireless Controller's Devices tab are grayed out. Turn it off to bind or unbind by hand; Nexus then binds only when you ask, and leaves fans bound to another PC alone.
- The Devices page's Available tab has a new OpenRGB Devices section, one row per device with its own Nexus Control switch. While Lighting is off, the section says so and offers Open Lighting.
- On the Available tab, USB Devices is now called Nexus Devices, and a device with Nexus Control off is dimmed.
- OpenRGB devices no longer show on the Lighting page or anywhere else while OpenRGB is not running, for example while Lighting is off.
- The LCD Flex page offers each fan's built-in effects for the whole fan or Per zone (Up and Down on a TL, Blades, Inner and Outer on an SL-INF), plus Merge all fans, Sync lighting across fans, Light sequence, Swap up and down, Reset lighting and Restart LCD. With the motherboard sync cable connected, Follow the motherboard ARGB plays your motherboard's colors instead.
- LCD Flex fans can also run Lighting page effects with Use Lighting page effects, where the zones of each fan sit together as one block. On the L-Wireless Controller's page, a Flex fan plugged in by USB shows as Connected by USB with Open LCD Flex page, and one still bound to the controller can be unbound there. Both fans can also be picked in an ARGB port chain.
- The Lian Li HydroShift II OLED Curved has a new Offline Mode switch in its Screen section, which shows the date and time on the display while your PC is off. With it on, the clock stays right after Nexus closes or the PC shuts down.
- NZXT F240 RGB Core fans on an NZXT controller such as the RGB & Fan Controller now light all 16 LEDs. A controller channel whose fans cannot be read can be given its own LED count, up to 40.
- On Windows, when Windows denies Nexus access to a cooler or screen LCD such as a Lian Li HydroShift II or Universal Screen 8.8, its page says Nexus can't access this device and asks you to close the maker's app. Nexus keeps trying and connects on its own.
- On macOS, turning Nexus Control off for a HYTE NP50 or HYTE THICC Q60 now also keeps OpenRGB from controlling it.

## Windows

- Nexus and its installer no longer run PowerShell, which removes a common reason antivirus apps flagged or blocked Nexus.
- A silent uninstall, for example from a script, no longer waits forever on Keep your Nexus data?. It keeps your data, and /DELETEDATA removes it.
- Nexus now checks who published a program before treating it as a conflicting app, so an unrelated program with the same file name is no longer flagged or shut down.

## Linux

- On KDE Plasma, the HYTE Y70's Orientation and Force display orientation settings now rotate its display, including when Nexus starts before the desktop is ready.

## Also in this release

- Nexus is now available in Czech. Pick Čeština under Settings > General > Language.

## Fixes

- After a crash or power loss, Nexus could start with every setting back at its default.
- A pinned device with a static look could stay dark instead of showing it, and changing one device's pin could blank the other pinned devices.
- A pinned keyboard with a static look never lit up keys for Type reactive.
- On a panel still on its starting layout, the Lighting widget's immersive view closed each time you picked a device.
- With Fast Startup on, a HYTE Q60 or Q80 screen stuck after starting the PC could go unrescued on later startups.
- On Windows, hiding a Web View widget on a HYTE Y70 or monitor panel with Rendering set to Desktop could leave a black square on the display.
- On Windows, with a HYTE Y70 or a monitor panel, moving the mouse past a monitor edge next to the panel could make the pointer jump to the middle of the main monitor, or bounce back instead of moving onto the panel.
- On Windows, FPS and Frame Time could show a dash until Nexus was restarted.
- An app you turned off in Manage conflicting applications, to keep it running, could be turned back on by another Nexus window showing an older list, and Nexus then shut it down.
- On a HYTE Keeb TKL, the Brightness slider under Firmware Lighting dimmed every other device too while a Lighting page effect ran. It now dims only the keyboard, and starts at 100% (an install still on the old 80% default moves to 100% once).
- On Lian Li wireless LCD fans, changing a screen's Rotation left a still picture or panel at the old angle until its content changed.
- An NZXT Kraken Plus V2 showed nothing on its display and was listed as a Kraken Elite V2.
- A Corsair Commander Core whose LED counts could not be read stopped OpenRGB from finding the devices after it, such as a second Commander Core.
- A Keychron keyboard could stay dark while Nexus controlled its lighting. Nexus now sets and saves full brightness on the keyboard when it takes over.
- OpenRGB skipped devices that took more than 5 seconds to detect.
- An OpenRGB device could be hidden while a similarly named device Nexus drives itself was connected, for example an original Lian Li Uni Hub next to a newer one.
- On Windows 10, Use as a secondary monitor could not install the display driver it needs, for example on a Lian Li Universal Screen 8.8.
- Fit entire image could fail to set a panel background for some picture and screen sizes, for example on a phone.
- On a round display, such as an NZXT Kraken's, a monitoring gauge that fills the display, like Ring, showed its value and label too small for the ring.
- A Ring gauge with Gauge colors set drew wider ends than a plain one, and a full one showed a notch where its ends meet.
- On a phone connected over Wi-Fi, the Nexus app could show Lost connection to your system while you browsed Lighting effects, even though the PC was fine.
- The OBS widget could show a longer stream time than the real one, for example when streaming more than one video track, and right after you pressed one of its buttons it could briefly show the old state again.
- The OBS widget's preview, shown before you add it, showed a live stream as if OBS were connected.
- Date pickers, such as the one in Screen Time, could open partly off screen in a short window.
- Open data folder and the Open folder buttons did nothing when the folder could not be opened. They now say Couldn't open the folder.
- In Settings > Profiles, a cloud backup that includes an app not installed on this PC showed the app's internal id instead of its name.
